Lenna Jawdat ’05

Since graduating from WIS, Lenna Jawdat has built a career at the intersection of writing, creativity, and healing. She is now a poet, writer, professor, and psychotherapist whose work explores trauma, identity, memory, and resilience. 


Her writing has appeared in publications including Poet Lore, The Margins, and Passengers Journal, and she was nominated for a 2021 Best of the Net award. In 2024, she received her MFA in Creative Writing from the Institute of American Indian Arts.

This summer, Lenna celebrated another major milestone: the publication of her debut poetry collection, 70,000. The book combines poetry, memoir, photography, maps, documents, and collage to explore cultural displacement and grief in Palestine.

 

Lenna now lives in Washington, DC, where she continues to write, teach, and practice psychotherapy.

 

Lenna’s favorite memories from WIS include reading in the Dacha and filling the teachers’ lounge with goodies on senior prank day. Her advice for anyone interested in creative writing is that it’s never too late. Anything you do in life makes you a better writer. Write in your free time and write because you love it, and doors will open.

Carmen Miller ’21

Carmen is pursuing a PhD in Comparative Literature at UMass Amherst. Working from a postcolonial lens, she studies early modern imperial literature from England and Spain. Instead of asking only how colonized peoples are changed by empire, her research asks what happens to the imperial center itself, and why imperialism depends on its citizens believing they’re untouched by it. 


She credits WIS with teaching her how to think rather than simply what to know, and with making her comfortable sitting with questions that don’t have easy answers. Theory of Knowledge was a big influence: TOK taught her that asking the best question can matter more than landing on one right answer.

 

At Haverford College, Carmen followed what interested her and found her home in Comparative Literature, which gave her more room to explore than a traditional English major. Teaching kept showing up along the way. She worked at the summer camp she’d attended as a kid, tutored, and TA’d for Haverford’s Spanish department. But it was during a semester abroad in Granada, Spain, the one stretch when she wasn’t teaching, that she realized how much she missed it. That’s when she decided to go to graduate school and work toward becoming a professor.

 

Her senior thesis put Salman Rushdie’s Haroun and the Sea of Stories in conversation with Juan Rulfo’s Pedro Páramo, a novel she first read in Sr. Antonio Ramallo’s class at WIS. Carmen explored how magical realism functions as a tool for resisting colonially imposed boundaries, considering the ways in which the novels subvert colonial understandings of physical space, language, and time in order to imagine more successful postcolonial futures. The thesis won a departmental award, she presented her research at the Northeast Modern Language Association (NeMLA) conference, and she graduated Phi Beta Kappa. Carmen is happy to talk with students and alumni about comparative literature, the college search, or Haverford!

 

Her favorite WIS memory is Parent Grill on Sports Day, and then going to the zoo with her friends afterwards. Her advice: Do the things you care about rather than worrying about what’s going to look the best on your résumé — you’ll learn so much by following the questions that matter to you, and being able to share about your passions is a great way to make connections.

JJ Tiziou ’97

Since graduating from WIS and the University of Pennsylvania, JJ Tiziou has built a career as an artist whose work brings people together. 


Based in Philadelphia, he is known for community portraiture and large-scale participatory projects, including How Philly Moves, an 85,000-square-foot mural at Philadelphia International Airport made from portraits of dancers from across the city.

 

Since 2016, JJ has walked the entire border of Philadelphia every year, and twice a year since 2020: a 100-plus-mile route through backyards, creek beds, cemeteries, industrial sites, and even across an airport runway! What began as a personal ritual has grown into Walk Around Philadelphia, a project that has brought more than 1,000 people together to walk stages of the route, with participants ranging in age from 11 to 96. JJ reserves 20% of spots for stipend-supported walkers, and the Walk has been offered in Spanish and French and adapted for programs at Penn, Drexel, and local schools.

 

This year, JJ received multi-year funding to train new facilitators and expand the project, with the hope that one day every young person in the region will have the chance to walk it. After 18 trips around the city, he says it still surprises him every time.

 

JJ’s favorite memory from WIS is of his first time in the darkroom, and of working on the yearbook. His advice for anyone interested in art, community organizing, or photography is to start by getting to know their neighbors, asking them what is important to them, and pushing that boundary of “who is my neighbor” a little bit further than what might be immediately comfortable.

Jim Zidar, Former Drama Instructor

We’re sad to share that Jim Zidar, who taught drama and directed productions at WIS for fourteen years, passed away at his home in Rockville, Maryland, on October 13, 2026. He was 70.

 

Many alumni will remember Jim from the stage and the rehearsal room. He was a working professional actor long before he came to WIS, and he stayed one throughout. He performed on stage, film, television, and radio, and with theaters across the DC area. In the local music scene, he was a well-known emcee and comedian who went by “The Big Red Thing,” and he could switch from Rodney Dangerfield to Elvis to “Ahnald” mid-sentence. After WIS, he spent twelve more years teaching stage production in DC classrooms and leading theater camps in Virginia and Maryland.

 

Jim’s family remembers him for his clever, inventive humor and his love of a good conversation or a friendly debate. He gave generously of himself to everyone around him. He is survived by his siblings Joan, Barbara, and John, along with many nieces, nephews, great-nieces and great-nephews, and longtime friends.

 

If you have a memory of Jim you’d like to share, reply to this email, and we’ll pass it along to his family.
