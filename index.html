WIS marks its 61st year in 2026-2027. In French tradition, the 61st year belongs to the plane tree — an image that meant learning long before it meant endurance. Socrates and Phaedrus settle beneath one to talk in the Phaedrus, while Hippocrates is said to have taught his students under a plane tree on Kos. Its bark sheds in plates each year and renews underneath, and it lines the roads and village squares of France. It is, above all, a tree people gather under. Three of them stand at Tregaron.

Katharine Morrow Segal '00

Since graduating from WIS, Katharine has built a career at the intersection of law and conservation. She is now Director & Associate General Counsel at the National Geographic Society, where she supports the Pristine Seas program. Prior to this role, Katharine spent over 11 years as in-house counsel at The Nature Conservancy, primarily supporting their Latin American & Caribbean programs. She is particularly excited to begin her new role on account of her passion for supporting cutting-edge exploration that highlights the wonders of the natural world around us.

When Katharine is not in the office, you can usually find her taking care of horses, hiking in the woods, or hanging out with her two kids. Recently, she's been really interested in getting back into oil painting by focusing on abstract landscapes!

 

Katharine's favorite memories from WIS relate to making friends from all around the world.

 

Her advice for any alumni or current students interested in conservation work is to remember that conservation organizations need people with all types of skills, not just scientists. So, you should pursue whatever field fits your skills (for her, it was law) and not worry about studying science (if it is not your jam!). Whether you become a lawyer, an accountant, an IT specialist, or a project manager, the conservation field will welcome you! It takes all types to run an organization that is trying to tackle global climate issues. Of course, we need the scientists too.

Rachele Riley '90

 

Rachele's career spans art, graphic design, and education. She is now an Associate Professor of New Media and Design at the University of North Carolina Greensboro, where her work explores visual representations of conflict, the poetics of land, water, and language, and the material systems of archives.

 

This spring, Rachele was named a Fulbright Scholar, receiving the Fulbright–Glasgow School of Art Distinguished Scholar Award. The award will take her to the United Kingdom and the Glasgow School of Art in Scotland, where she'll research water ecologies, mythologies, and letterform design across Glasgow and the Scottish Highlands. Her project, "World Wide Water: Visualizing the Dynamics of Water, Letterforms, and Space," will result in a visual mapping and multimedia image series inspired by the region's rivers, lochs, waterways, and design histories, and she'll also teach within the School of Design at the Glasgow School of Art.

 

Rachele's favorite memories from WIS are of her friends and teachers, as well as the unique spaces and creative learning activities at WIS. For example, she recalls a hands-on data study and mapping assignment in a geography class with Mrs. Vargo in which the class analyzed the drop in outside temperature as one moved away from the city — from downtown DC to Silver Spring, MD. Another memorable experience was reading Jean Anouilh’s Antigone in Mme. Heidelbroek’s French class and discussing existential themes in the text. Also formative was learning how to develop and articulate experimental methods in Chemistry, discussing the limits of memory in TOK class, reading literary works in depth in English, and singing as an ensemble in class with Mr. Vincent.

 

Rachele's advice for anyone interested in art and design is to find inspiration for your art wherever possible in all things you do, feel, and experience. Continue to be curious as you move through life, and balance planning and looking ahead with being present and open in the moment. Whatever your vision or interest, follow it and persevere.
